AP Travel

Which country, when, and who will come.

Air seats · exchange rates · search volume · holiday signals, combined to show where the opportunity is: which country → which city · which guest · which week.

01 · One scene

When signals align, an opportunity appears.

Only markets where exchange rates, air capacity and search volume point the same way are raised, with evidence. Budget, channel and message stay with the marketer.

02 · What it watches

Six signals. Only when they agree.

Air seats · exchange rates · search volume · arrivals · holidays · concert schedules. One or two looking good is not enough. When all six point to the same place, it becomes an opportunity.

03 · Market size

The year 20 million arrive. Who comes, and when, differs.

2.09 million visitors in July 2026, +20.8% year on year. China · Japan · Taiwan · United States · Hong Kong, in that order. Each country has its own peak season, spend and reason to come.

Figures are refreshed after each monthly statistics release. Estimates are illustrative, not results.

04 · Who uses it

Anyone waiting for guests.

Hotel and resort marketing, local governments and tourism boards, travel agencies and OTAs, concert and MICE organizers. It answers “which country do we advertise in now” every week.

05 · How it connects

The same engine as AP Revenue. Only the data differs.

Signals → priority → human decision → outcome on record. Public data (Korea Tourism Data Lab · TourAPI) and partner data feed a weekly “this week's opportunities” list and campaign briefs.

  • Public · partner data
  • Six signals normalized
  • Opportunity list (origin → city · guest · week)
  • Manager decides
  • Campaign
  • Verified by visits and bookings
