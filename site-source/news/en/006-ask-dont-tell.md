You have probably felt uneasy when an AI only praised your idea. In that situation we tack on "Don't flatter me" or "Evaluate it coldly." But the effect does not last. Instructions fade as a conversation gets longer, and sometimes the AI tilts the other way and cuts everything down too harshly.

## Why it doesn't work

AI writes **what would naturally follow my sentence** rather than judging my sentence. After "My plan is good, right?", "Yes, it's good" follows naturally. Adding "Tell me honestly" leaves the skeleton of the sentence unchanged. The article we referred to cites research from the UK AI Safety Institute, Stanford and others, saying that sycophancy dropped sharply when the same claim was put in question form. We have not checked these studies against the originals either, so we do not repeat the figures. The principle, though, is something you can test yourself.

## How to change it — example sentences we use

- Assertion: "This price list is reasonable. Right?" → Question: "Where might this price list look unreasonable?"
- Assertion: "This wording is the best, isn't it?" → Question: "Where would someone seeing these two wordings for the first time get stuck on each?"
- Assertion: "I wrote this, so fix it." → Question: "Which sentences in this piece have weak support?" (Leaving out that it is mine makes the evaluation less generous.)
- Assertion: "We'll be fine going with this plan, right?" → Question: "If this plan had failed three months from now, what are the three most likely causes?"

## Three principles

1. **Don't put my conclusion at the front of the sentence.**
2. **Remove the information that it is mine.** Delete "that I made."
3. **Have it imagine failure first.** A question asking for counter-evidence leaves little room for flattery to creep in.

The very fact that you feel good when AI agrees with you is the heart of the problem. The better it feels, the more reason to ask once more.
